\paragraph{Automated evaluator sensitivity.}
To examine sensitivity to automated semantic judgments,
we evaluated four locally hosted language-model judges
on 263 blinded action-proposal cases. Excluding four
previously discussed calibration examples leaves 259
cases for comparative analysis. All four judges
completed the evaluation without unresolved cases.

Agreement with the frozen deterministic oracle on
effect equivalence was 81.85\% for GPT-OSS 20B,
75.29\% for Qwen3.5 35B, 66.41\% for Llama 3.3
70B, and 64.48\% for Mistral 24B. More
importantly, agreement on the 93 oracle-positive
equivalence cases was only 55.91\%, 38.71\%,
6.45\%, and 6.45\%, respectively.

Pairwise three-label Cohen's $\kappa$ for effect
equivalence ranged from 0.027 to 0.344, revealing
substantial evaluator dependence. Forbiddenness
judgments were generally more concordant with the
machine oracle, but agreement varied by evaluator.

These results measure sensitivity to model-generated
judgments, not accuracy against independently
adjudicated human ground truth. Differences in judge
prompts also prevent attributing all variation solely
to model architecture. Accordingly, we retain the
deterministic oracle as the frozen primary instrument
and treat AI-judge disagreement as a construct-validity
diagnostic requiring independent human assessment.
